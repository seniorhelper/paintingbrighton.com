Four very different clients

Commercial work around Brighton is unusually varied, and each type needs a different plan.

  • Main Street Brighton. Older masonry storefronts, pedestrian frontage, owner-operated businesses that cannot lose a Saturday. Evening and overnight interiors; contained, phased exteriors.
  • Warehouse and distribution. Brighton and the US-85 and I-76 corridors hold a lot of industrial space. Big wall areas and tall ceilings, where the coating is chosen for forklift scuffs, washdown or brighter light rather than looks.
  • Agricultural facilities. Processing buildings, equipment shops and storage structures. Different substrates, different access, and operations that run on their own schedule rather than ours.
  • HOA and multi-family. The newer subdivisions have community buildings, perimeter fencing and phased exterior programs with boards, submissions and resident notification.

How a commercial estimate is built

The walkthrough is mostly questions. When are you quietest? Which doors do deliveries use? Does your lease restrict contractor hours? Is there a wall shared with the unit next door? None of that is on a drawing and all of it decides the plan.

What comes back is a document you can hand to a landlord or a board: surfaces listed individually, the preparation each gets, product and sheen by substrate, coat counts, the phases and which nights we are on site. The certificate of insurance arrives before anyone turns up, matched to whatever your lease actually requires.

You deal with one named contact from estimate to final walkthrough.

HOA communities

Community repaints run on board calendars and color approvals more than on paint. That process has its own page: HOA painting in Brighton.

Older masonry downtown

A fair amount of Brighton's Main Street frontage is genuinely old brick. Masonry generally needs a breathable coating rather than standard exterior paint, and buildings predating 1978 carry the same lead-safe requirements a house does. Both are worth knowing before you collect bids.

Questions

Commercial and ag questions

What commercial painting do you handle in Brighton?

Main Street retail and restaurants, offices, warehouse and light industrial around Brighton, farm and processing buildings, and HOA and multi-family properties.

Can you work after hours?

Yes, that is standard. Most small business interiors here are done evenings and overnight, and larger properties are phased so only one area is affected at a time.

Do you provide a certificate of insurance?

Yes. It arrives before the first day on site, with whatever coverage limits and named insureds your lease or property manager asks for.

Do you paint agricultural facilities?

Yes. Processing buildings, equipment shops and storage structures, with coatings chosen for the substrate and the working conditions rather than appearance alone.

How do you handle HOA color approvals?

We put together the color samples and paperwork the board needs and suggest submitting over the winter, since board review is usually what holds up a summer repaint.

Can you paint an older brick storefront?

Yes. Brick and block that old wants a coating that lets vapor through rather than a standard exterior product, and anything put up before 1978 carries the same federal lead-safe obligations a dwelling would.
